Questions

Reasonable things to ask.

Do I need an account?
No. Chronos has no sign-up and no server holding your data. A countdown is stored in your browser and encoded into its own share link, so the link is the countdown. That also means clearing your browser data clears your library — save any link you care about.
Does the link expire?
Never. The whole countdown — date, timezone, look, colour — is packed into the URL itself. As long as this site is online, the link works, and it keeps working whether the event is next year or last Tuesday.
What happens when the countdown reaches zero?
It celebrates, then keeps going: with "count up" switched on, the display flips to showing how long it has been since the moment. Turn the celebration off if you'd rather it stayed quiet.
Can I put it on my own website?
Yes, but only for a page you control. Open any countdown, choose Embed, and paste the snippet — it's an <iframe> pointing at the share link. If you're not sure your site allows embeds, the plain link always works.

The manual

How to get the most out of it.

Short answers, no fluff. Everything here is doable from the create page.

Sharing

Hit Copy link and send it anywhere. The entire countdown travels inside the URL, so there's no account, no database and nothing to expire. Anyone who opens it sees the same instant.

Embedding

Choose Embed to get an <iframe> snippet. Paste it into any page you control. Set a fixed height — 360px suits the digits and bold styles; 300px for compact.

Timezones

Set the zone the event truly happens in. A 7pm lamp-lighting in Delhi stays 7pm in Delhi; someone in London opening the link sees it as 1:30pm their time — correctly.

Calendars

Add to calendar downloads an .ics file with the event's start and end. Open it and your calendar app takes it from there — a handy way to remember your own deadline.

Full-screen mode

Open full screen gives you a clean stage with no header or footer — ideal for a projector, a TV at a party, or a kiosk screen at an event.
